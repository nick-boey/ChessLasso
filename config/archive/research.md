# Training evidence register

Seeded 2026-11-01; implementation decisions are hypotheses to assess through independent retention and transfer.

- Charness et al. (2005), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/acp.1106: serious study associates with chess expertise. Observational; no guaranteed rating/timetable. Use purposeful practice, not a promised path to1800.
- Roediger and Karpicke (2006), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/: retrieval practice supports delayed retention in a non-chess setting. Chess application is an inference; retest in different unlabelled positions.
- Shute (2008), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654307313795: manageable, specific feedback after an attempt; timing depends on learner needs. Preserve productive thought, ask when the cause of difficulty is unclear, explain prerequisites directly when needed.
- ManyClasses (2021), https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/25152459211027575/: no universal immediate-feedback advantage across studied classes. Do not impose a universal hint timer.
- Sinha and Kapur (2021), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105: carefully designed problem solving followed by instruction can support understanding. Do not abandon a beginner without prerequisites.
- Chess Steps, https://www.stappenmethode.nl/en/info/workbooks: established pedagogy combines instruction, exercises, games and discussion, including mixed problems. Teaching practice, not controlled evidence for this application.
- Lichess accuracy, https://lichess.org/page/accuracy: position context affects accuracy. Engine accuracy and bot results do not establish FIDE strength.

Record source/date, claim, evidence type, limitations and the training decision for each addition. Keep time controls, assistance, notation friction and observed exposure separate. Foreground time is not measured attention. Small samples need uncertainty and a decision to continue gathering evidence.
